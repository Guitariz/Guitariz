## Why Musicians Look for Chord AI Alternatives

The original Chord AI app is impressive, but it has several key limitations that push users to look elsewhere:

- **App-Only**: Chord AI is only available as a mobile app for iOS and Android. There is no web version. If you are working on a desktop computer or laptop, you are out of luck.
- **Paywalled Chord Types**: Basic major and minor chords are available free, but 7th chords, 9th chords, diminished, augmented, and slash chords (the ones that actually matter in jazz, pop, and R&B) require a paid subscription starting at $3.99/month.
- **No MIDI Export**: Chord AI does not export detected chord progressions as MIDI files. This is a major limitation for producers who want to import chords directly into Ableton, FL Studio, or Logic Pro.
- **No Stem Separation**: The app does not isolate vocals or instruments before analysis. On dense, production-heavy mixes, this limits chord detection accuracy significantly.

## 2. Chordify

Chordify is a well-known chord transcription website. It has a large catalog of pre-analyzed YouTube tracks and allows you to upload your own files.

### What it does well:
- Large pre-existing song catalog means many popular tracks are already analyzed
- Simple, clean interface for beginners
- Beat and tempo detection is built-in

### The Catch:
While the basic version is free, it restricts key features behind a monthly premium paywall. In the free version:
- You cannot transpose songs
- You cannot edit wrong chords
- You cannot download MIDI files
- The interface is heavily cluttered with banner ads that break the practice flow

Chordify is best for casual, occasional use with mainstream pop songs. Power users who need chord transposition and MIDI export will quickly hit frustrating paywalls.

## 3. AnthemScore (Desktop Software)

AnthemScore is professional-grade desktop software that uses machine learning to transcribe audio files into full sheet music and MIDI.

### What it does well:
- Outputs full piano roll-style sheet music notation
- Detects individual notes in polyphonic recordings
- One-time purchase license (no recurring subscription)

### The Catch:
Unlike browser-based tools, AnthemScore requires downloading and installing software on a PC or Mac. It is also not free — it offers a 30-day trial, after which you must purchase a lifetime license starting at $29.

For most guitarists who just need chord detection (not full sheet music), this is overkill and an unnecessary expense.

## How to Get the Most Accurate Chord Detection

Regardless of which tool you use, here are three techniques that dramatically improve chord detection accuracy:

### 1. Use a High-Quality Source File
Always use the highest quality audio file available. A 320kbps MP3 or lossless FLAC will produce much better chromagram analysis than a 128kbps file. Streaming-ripped files often have compression artifacts that confuse pitch detection algorithms.

### 2. Pre-Process with Stem Separation
On vocal-heavy or heavily produced tracks, the lead vocal melody often "bleeds" into the harmonic analysis and causes the algorithm to misidentify chords. Using the [Vocal Splitter](https://guitariz.studio/vocal-splitter) to isolate just the instrumental layer before running chord analysis can dramatically improve accuracy — especially on pop and hip-hop tracks where the 808 bass and the vocal share a lot of harmonic space.

### 3. Cross-Reference with the Key
Once you have the detected key (use the [Key Detector](https://guitariz.studio/key-detector)), apply basic music theory: in any given key, only 7 diatonic chords naturally occur. If the algorithm reports a C# Major chord in a song that is clearly in C Major, it is almost certainly a detection error. The actual chord is probably E Minor or A Minor. Understanding the key dramatically speeds up your error-correction.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I use these tools to detect chords from a YouTube video?**

A: Yes. The most straightforward method is to download the audio from the YouTube video (using any YouTube-to-MP3 converter), then upload the audio file into [Guitariz Chord AI](https://guitariz.studio/chord-ai) for analysis.

**Q: How accurate is AI chord detection on complex songs?**

A: Modern AI chord detection is highly accurate on straightforward pop, folk, country, and acoustic music. Accuracy drops somewhat on:
- Very dense orchestral recordings
- Heavily distorted electric guitar with lots of palm muting
- Songs that use non-diatonic or chromatic chord substitutions frequently

For these genres, using stem separation to isolate piano or acoustic guitar before analysis helps significantly.

**Q: Can these tools detect slash chords like C/E or G/B?**

A: Guitariz Chord AI detects slash chords and displays the bass note separately from the chord quality. This is critical for accurate piano arrangement and bass line transcription.

**Q: Do I need to create an account to use Guitariz?**

A: No. Guitariz is completely account-free. Simply visit [guitariz.studio/chord-ai](https://guitariz.studio/chord-ai), upload your file, and start detecting chords instantly. No email address, no password, no credit card.

**Q: Can I export chords as a PDF or lead sheet?**

A: Guitariz currently exports chord progressions as MIDI files, which can be imported into any notation software (like MuseScore, Sibelius, or Finale) to generate a lead sheet. Native PDF export with chord symbols is a feature in development.

**Q: What is the difference between chord detection and tablature (tab)?**

A: Chord detection identifies the harmonic content of a recording — what chord is playing at each moment. Tablature (tab) specifies exactly which strings and frets to press for a specific guitar arrangement. Chord detection gives you the harmonic map; translating that into tab depends on the specific guitar voicing you choose.
