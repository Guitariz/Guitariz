## What Is BPM and Why Does It Matter?

**BPM** stands for *Beats Per Minute*. It measures the tempo (speed) of a musical piece by counting how many quarter-note beats occur in 60 seconds.

- **60–80 BPM**: Ballads, lofi hip-hop, downtempo, slow blues
- **90–115 BPM**: Classic rock, pop, hip-hop, R&B
- **120–130 BPM**: House music, EDM, modern dance pop
- **140–160 BPM**: Trap, dubstep, fast rock, punk
- **170–180 BPM**: Drum & bass, metal blast beats

Having an exact BPM reading enables you to:
1. **Time-stretch audio clips without pitch distortion** in your DAW.
2. **Synchronize delay, reverb, and modulation effects** to the song's rhythmic grid.
3. **Lock in drum loops and backing tracks** for focused instrument practice.
4. **Beatmatch seamlessly** when performing live sets.

## Understanding Half-Time and Double-Time Ambiguity

A common question when detecting BPM is whether a track is **70 BPM or 140 BPM**.

- In a trap song produced at **140 BPM**, the snare typically hits on beat 3 (half-time feel).
- Some listeners count this as **70 BPM** (snare on beats 2 and 4).
- Both are musically valid! Modern beat detectors allow you to toggle between `1x`, `0.5x` (half-time), and `2x` (double-time) to match your workflow.
